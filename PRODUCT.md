# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary decision-maker:** the owner or general manager of an *established* home-services
business — HVAC, plumbing, or electrical — roughly 5–50 employees / 3+ trucks, already
running a field-service platform (Housecall Pro, Jobber, ServiceTitan). They are
operationally sharp but not "software people"; they adopted an FSM system because running
at their size demanded it, not because they enjoy tools. When they hit this site they are
time-starved and skeptical, often arriving from a cold call, a cold email, or a live-demo
follow-up, and frequently reading on a phone between jobs.

**Job to be done:** stop bleeding revenue to missed calls and slow follow-up *and* relieve
the crushing office/admin labor load — without handing control to an AI they don't trust.
Their mental comparison is "another AI tool" on one side and "hiring another office
employee" on the other.

**Secondary:** office managers / operations leads who feel the admin pain daily and
influence the decision.

**Future (not addressed by current copy):** med spa, dental, legal — expansion markets, not
today's audience.

## Product Purpose

OutboundOS is a **managed AI Workforce** for home-service businesses: a team of specialized
AI employees (receptionist, follow-up specialist, review manager, and more over time) that
act on the business's real context through a shared **Business Brain**, under human
oversight. Owners *hire* the workforce; they never log in to configure software.

The website's single job is to convert a skeptical, time-starved owner into a booked
30-minute demo (via Calendly: https://calendly.com/francisco-r-outboundos/30min). It does
that by making one thing believable above all else: **this is AI you can actually trust to
run your front office** — because it knows your business, and every decision it makes is
logged and reversible.

Success = qualified demo bookings from established shops. The site sells **outcomes**
(recovered revenue, reclaimed time, lower labor cost), not "AI." It is explicitly **not** a
CRM, **not** a GoHighLevel clone, **not** a chatbot.

## Brand Personality

**Direct · credible · grounded.** The voice of a founder who builds systems, not a marketer
selling hype. It speaks the owner's language, respects their skepticism, and proves rather
than promises — confident without shouting.

Emotional goals: **relief** (someone finally gets this), **trust** (I can hand this over
safely), and **control** (I'm still in charge). Never hype, never condescending, never
tech-bro.

## Anti-references

- **Generic blue SaaS template** — the site's own previous look; the default AI-automation
  aesthetic. The thing we are escaping.
- **Hype-y marketing agency** — gradients everywhere, "CRUSH your revenue," inflated claims.
- **Cartoonish AI chatbot** — robot mascots, playful speech bubbles, toy-like.
- **GoHighLevel-clone dashboard** — busy, generic agency-tool aesthetics.
- **Saturated AI "editorial" lane** — display-serif-italic + mono labels + ruled columns,
  and the reflex blue-on-white tech-minimal default. Distinctiveness over safe.

## Design Principles

1. **Trust is shown, not claimed.** Every trust statement pairs with visible proof — the
   Business Brain knowing the business, decisions logged and reversible, real mechanics on
   screen. This is the site's spine.
2. **Speak the owner's world.** Ground everything in trucks, dispatch, missed calls, and
   payroll — not abstract "automation." "We get your world" beats "cutting-edge AI."
3. **Sell the outcome; name the AI second.** Lead with reclaimed time, recovered revenue,
   lower labor cost. The AI is *how*, not *what*.
4. **Hire, don't configure.** Reinforce a managed workforce they hire — never software they
   must learn. No dashboard-anxiety.
5. **Distinctive over safe.** Reject the category-default blue-SaaS look; a skeptical buyer
   trusts a brand with a clear point of view over a generic one.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum (≥4.5:1 body, ≥3:1 large text). Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` with
crossfade/instant fallbacks for all entrance and marquee motion. Maintain both light and
dark themes. Real semantic interactive elements, labeled form inputs, full keyboard
operability of nav, CTAs, and the contact form. Mobile-first — the primary visitor is on a
phone between jobs.
